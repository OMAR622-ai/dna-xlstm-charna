# 50 questions and short answers about the project

State: 5 October 2026. See [EXPLAINED.md](EXPLAINED.md) for the long explanation.

## The goal

1. **What is the project about?**
   Training a small AI model to "read" human DNA, using ChaRNABERT's design (GBST + BERT), and
   testing whether its learned way of reading helps.

2. **What question do we want to answer?**
   Does GBST (learning how to cut DNA into blocks) make a small model better than reading one
   base at a time?

3. **Where does the model design come from?**
   From ChaRNABERT (Morales-Pastor et al., 2024), an RNA model. We apply it to DNA.

4. **Where does the training recipe come from?**
   From the DNA-xLSTM / Bio-xLSTM paper (Schmidinger et al., 2024), so our results compare
   with theirs.

5. **Why compare with DNA-xLSTM?**
   It is a published set of small DNA models trained the same way, with known results.

6. **What is a "foundation model"?**
   A model first trained on lots of unlabelled sequence, then reused for many specific tasks.

7. **Why does the model need no labels?**
   The sequence is its own teacher: we hide bases and the model must guess them.

8. **What could this be useful for later?**
   Predicting promoters, enhancers, splice sites or histone marks from DNA alone (the NT
   benchmark, postponed for now).

## The model

9. **What are the four parts of the model?**
   Embedding (letters → numbers), GBST (learned reading), BERT encoder (bases exchange
   information), prediction head (guess each base).

10. **What is GBST?**
    Gradient-Based Subsequence Tokenization: at every position it builds blocks of 1–4 bases
    and learns how much to trust each.

11. **How many candidate blocks per position?**
    10: one of size 1, two of size 2, three of size 3, four of size 4 (all blocks that contain
    the position).

12. **Why several blocks of the same size?**
    Different starting points, like reading frames of a gene.

13. **How does GBST "choose"?**
    A scorer gives each block a score; softmax turns scores into weights; the output is the
    weighted mix — a soft choice, learned by training.

14. **What is score calibration?**
    Positions with similar weight patterns pull each other's weights together, so the reading
    style stays consistent along the sequence.

15. **Does GBST shorten the sequence?**
    No. Charformer did; ChaRNABERT removed it to keep one output per base, and so do we.

16. **What is BERT?**
    A Transformer encoder that reads in both directions and is trained by guessing hidden tokens.

17. **What is attention?**
    Every base looks at every other base and collects information from the relevant ones.

18. **What do RoPE, QKNorm and SwiGLU do?**
    RoPE tells the model distances between bases; QKNorm keeps training stable; SwiGLU is the
    small network inside each block.

19. **How big is our model?**
    2,107,392 parameters: 256 numbers per base, 4 encoder blocks, 8 attention heads.

20. **Why so small when ChaRNABERT has 8–650 M?**
    To match the 2 M models of the DNA-xLSTM paper for a fair comparison.

## The comparison model

21. **What is Transformer++?**
    The same kind of encoder but reading one base at a time with a lookup table — no GBST.

22. **Why train it?**
    It is the control group: if GBST-BERT beats it, GBST is the likely reason.

23. **Is it identical except for GBST?**
    Almost: it also uses RMSNorm and no QKNorm (DNA-xLSTM's version), and its own learning rate.

## Training

24. **What game does the model play?**
    Masked language modelling: 15% of bases are hidden and must be guessed.

25. **What happens to the hidden bases?**
    80% become [MASK], 10% a random token, 10% stay unchanged (BERT's recipe).

26. **What is the loss?**
    How wrong the guesses are (cross-entropy). Lower is better; 1.386 = random guessing.

27. **What is one training step?**
    The model reads 1,024 sequences of 1,024 bases (about 1 million bases) and updates itself once.

28. **How many steps?**
    Full run 10,000 steps (10.5 billion bases); small experiment 500 steps.

29. **What is the learning rate?**
    How big each update is. GBST-BERT uses 2e-3 (best in our check); Transformer++ uses 1e-2.

30. **Why not ChaRNABERT's 5e-4?**
    In our check, 2e-3 gave a clearly lower loss (1.154 vs 1.194 after 500 steps).

31. **What is reverse-complement augmentation?**
    Half the time the model sees the other DNA strand, so it learns both strands are equivalent.

32. **Why fp32 and not bf16?**
    Our GPUs (GTX 1080 Ti, RTX 2080 Ti, GV100) do not support bf16.

## The data

33. **Which DNA?**
    The human reference genome hg38: about 3.03 billion bases.

34. **How is it split?**
    Training 2.33 B unique bases (77%), validation 0.29 B (10%), test 0.27 B (9%).

35. **Does the model see the test DNA during training?**
    No. Test DNA is only used to measure the final accuracy.

36. **How often does the full run see the training DNA?**
    About 4.5 times on average (10.5 B bases read / 2.33 B unique).

37. **What about N (unknown bases)?**
    They are ignored: never hidden-and-graded, never a target.

38. **Why is the full genome not on GitHub?**
    It is 3.1 GB; GitHub refuses files over 100 MB. A download script and a 7.9 MB sample are there.

## Results so far

39. **How good was the small experiment (0.5 B bases)?**
    About 44% of hidden bases guessed correctly on unseen DNA, for both models.

40. **What do 25% and 29% mean in the table?**
    25% = random guessing; ~29% = always guessing the most common base. A real model must beat both.

41. **Who won the small experiment?**
    A tie: GBST-BERT 43.94% vs Transformer++ 43.77% — too small a difference to call.

42. **Why did GBST-BERT start slower?**
    It must first learn *how* to read before it can use it; it caught up by 0.3 B bases.

43. **What did the full run show so far?**
    Validation loss 1.056 after 2.1 B bases — close to the published models (≈0.96–1.03).

44. **What loss would be "good"?**
    Around 1.0 after the full run, like the published 2 M models; below Transformer++ would show GBST helps.

45. **Is the comparison fully fair?**
    Not completely: GBST-BERT's learning rate was tuned here, Transformer++'s comes from the paper.

## Practical

46. **Where does the training run?**
    On the JKU server student07 (2× RTX 2080 Ti + 2× Quadro GV100), moved from student01 on 5 Oct.

47. **What if the training stops?**
    It saves a checkpoint every 500 steps and can continue from it (tested).

48. **When are the final results expected?**
    GBST-BERT ≈ Tue 6 Oct morning; Transformer++ and the final accuracy table ≈ Tue evening/night.

49. **Where is everything saved?**
    GitHub, branch `charna-gbst`: code, models (`charna_project/models/`), results, one page per
